# Lesson 20 — Motorola 68000

## Mission

Apply modern reversing methodology to a classic 68k target and prepare for Amiga binaries.

## Register model

The 68000 exposes two visibly different register groups:

- data registers `D0-D7`;
- address registers `A0-A7`.

`A7` is the stack pointer.

Do not assume a register's semantic role solely from its group. Infer role from use and calling convention.

## Operand sizes

Instructions commonly make width explicit:

- `.B` — byte;
- `.W` — word;
- `.L` — long.

Track width carefully. It matters for arithmetic, comparisons, sign extension and structure recovery.

## Addressing modes

Become comfortable recognizing:

- register direct;
- address-register indirect;
- displacement;
- indexed forms;
- immediate values;
- absolute addresses;
- PC-relative addressing.

The richness of 68k addressing modes means one instruction can express work that would require several instructions on a simpler load/store ISA.

## Condition codes and branches

Recover comparisons and CFG edges from condition-code-setting instructions and conditional branches.

As always:

1. identify compared values;
2. identify signed/unsigned interpretation;
3. map true and false successors;
4. assign semantic meaning only afterward.

## Calls

Recognize subroutine call/return mechanisms and trace stack/register effects. Compiler conventions and operating-system conventions matter more than a memorized generic pattern.

## Amiga bridge

68k knowledge alone is not enough to reverse an Amiga program. You also need to understand:

- Amiga executable/container format;
- library/device calling conventions;
- resident/system structures;
- relocations;
- symbols/debug information when present.

The next specialist lesson introduces Amiga Hunk.

## Principle

**The 68k syntax looks different; the evidence questions are the same.**
